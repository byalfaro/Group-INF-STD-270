Digital Collections & Imaging Services

Overview

We interviewed Mario Einaudi, Head of Digital Collections & Imaging Services at The Huntington Library, Art Museum, and Botanical Gardens. Digital Collections & Imaging Services is responsible for the digitization and digital reproduction of materials in Huntington collections for exhibitions and the online catalog to support the institution’s mission of education and public access. Einaudi manages and assists department staff, the Huntington Digital Library, and interdepartmental interactions. To be successful in digital collections, strong technical skills in metadata, cataloging, digital asset management systems, and content management systems are important. For imaging, a professional photographer skillset and knowledge of standards like FADGI (Federal Agencies Digital Guidelines Initiative) are needed. Expertise in these areas ensures an accurate capture of objects for digitization. Additionally, a strong leader in this field understands the institution’s mission, supports and protects staff, takes risks, and advocates for department needs. Professional development happens as on-the-job training, outside programming, and professional networking. If on-site professional development is not offered, programs like the Northeast Document Conservation Center’s Digital Directions provide opportunities to develop skills for digital projects and project management. However, paying for outside training can be difficult on a librarian salary. For students, internships can be difficult to find. Therefore, working on small projects is one way to learn as much as possible. Einaudi describes the field as static. Ongoing challenges include funding, unpaid internships, contract positions, a tense political environment, and a lack of understanding about library professions. To combat this, according to him, librarians need to be better about advocating for themselves and teaching people about what happens behind the scenes. Lastly, while The Huntington does not currently have an AI policy, one is under development. AI is beginning to influence areas, like handwriting transcription and 3D capture, but it cannot replace imaging services, metadata specialists, and catalogers. For these, only humans can capture accurate and descriptive data.

Work in digital collections requires strong technical skills including metadata creation, cataloging, digital asset management systems (DAMS), and content management systems (CMS). Imaging specialists must also understand photography standards such as FADGI to accurately capture collection materials.

Professional development often occurs through networking, conferences, and specialized training programs like NEDCC Digital Directions, though these are often self-funded.

According to Einaudi, the field is relatively static. Challenges include funding limitations, contract positions, and unpaid internships. AI technologies may assist with transcription and 3D capture but cannot replace human expertise in metadata and imaging work.

Typical Tasks

Digital collections professionals digitize physical materials, manage born-digital content, and provide online access to collections.

Typical responsibilities include:

• Creating descriptive metadata

• Editing and processing digital images

• Managing digital asset management systems

• Supporting digitization requests

• Maintaining long-term digital preservation workflows

Professional Context

Common professional organizations include:

• Society of American Archivists (SAA)

• American Library Association (ALA)

• Digital Library Federation (DLF)

These organizations provide conferences, mentoring programs, publications, and job boards.

Blogs and communities like ArchivesGig and Code4Lib Jobs are important resources for job listings and networking.

Job-Market Outlook

The field of librarianship and digital collections is growing slowly. Labor assessments show around a 2% growth rate in librarianship positions.

Many institutions rely on temporary contracts for digitization projects, which can create instability for workers.

However, digital roles involving data management and digital asset systems are becoming more common.

Technical Skill Snapshot

Important entry-level skills include:

• Metadata creation

• Cataloging

• Digital asset management systems

• Imaging and digitization standards

• Database management

• Project management

Some positions also require design tools such as Adobe Creative Suite.

Training Agenda

Recommended courses include:

• Metadata

• Cataloging

• Database Management

• Digital Asset Management

• Data Ethics

Internships involving digitization workflows or digital collections management provide valuable experience.

Labor Trends

Common issues in GLAM institutions include temporary contracts, limited funding, and unpaid internships.

These conditions create barriers for many entering the profession, especially in high-cost areas.

Advocacy efforts focus on improving pay, reducing contract positions, and increasing institutional investment in digital infrastructure.
